# Task: sing

The accurate English of this unit already exists; the input gives it, line by line, with its word-by-word gloss. It carries the meaning, and it stays as it is. Your task is a second English beside it: **the song as it would be sung.** Readers who cannot hear the source should still get what the source gives a listener: its shape, its rhymes, its refrain coming round again, the voice of the singer, the wonder and the sting of its images.

The whole edition rests on the accurate English. This version is its companion and never its replacement: it must say what the accurate English says, keep every image, and add nothing. For this task the SYSTEM rule "do not paraphrase" relaxes in one way only: inside a line you may reorder words, compress, and choose the plainer or the more singable of two English words for the same thing.

**What a sung version is not:** the accurate English with its words contracted. If a line could stand unchanged in the accurate translation, it has not been sung yet. The accurate English already exists; a second copy of it adds nothing. The engine flags any line left identical to the accurate English, and any line of more than about 13 syllables.

## The rules

1. **Same shape.** One English line for each half-line of the source, in the same order, couplet by couplet. The refrain stays the refrain: the same English words every time it is sung. The poet's self-naming ("Kāṇha says…") stays in its couplet.
2. **Every image, none added, none explained.** The boat stays a boat, Ḍombī stays Ḍombī, the lotus has its sixty-four petals. Never put an esoteric reading into a line: what the commentary says an image means belongs in its notes, not in the song. Words a glossary entry lists under "never in a line" must not appear. Paradox stays paradox.
3. **The meaning stays the accurate English's meaning.** Where the accurate draft flags a line as open to two senses, you may sing either; say which in that couplet's `kept`. If you believe the accurate English is wrong, sing it as it stands and raise the point in `questions`.
4. **Rhyme honestly or not at all.** The sound profile shows which couplets rhyme in the source and on what sound. Where English offers a rhyme without bending the sense, take it: a full rhyme, a slant rhyme, or an echo of vowel or consonant on the last strong word of each line. Moving the key word to the end of the line is allowed. Adding a word for its rhyme is not, and neither is twisting the syntax ("the town without"). A couplet that cannot rhyme honestly stays unrhymed, and its `letGo` says so. A forced rhyme is a worse loss than a missing one.
5. **Sing shorter.** A sung line is almost always shorter than the accurate one. Cut what songs cut: articles, "that" and "there", "I have" and "it is", auxiliaries, prepositions that only explain. Keep every noun that is an image and every verb that acts. Give each line about four strong beats (rarely five) and let the weak syllables fall where English puts them, and keep the two halves of a couplet close in length. Say each line aloud in your head, at the pace of a song, before you keep it.
6. **Keep the sounds that carry the song.** A name that rings through the song (Ḍombī), a word repeated or doubled in the source (*choi choi*), a call ("ālo", "hālo"), the refrain: repetition in the source is repetition in the English, with the same English word each time. Where a couplet cannot rhyme honestly, give it another of the sounds this poetry lives on: a word or name struck twice, alliteration, a chime inside the line, the two halves built alike. The refrain especially must be easy to sing back: short, strongly beaten, the same every time.
7. **Find the voice, then keep it.** These are songs of wandering yogis: by turns teasing, erotic, fierce, riddling, rapt, blunt. Decide whose voice each song is in and to whom it is sung, and name it in `voice`. Plain living English: no archaisms (thee, lo, verily), no "O" before a name, no hymn diction, no Dharma vocabulary the source does not use. A call like "hey" keeps its bite.
8. **Glossary terms.** You may mark terms with `[surface]{term-id}`; it is allowed, not required. A `keep-source` term stays in its source form (Ḍombī). Doctrinal terms (mind, great bliss, emptiness, the innate, the guru) keep their approved English exactly, so the teaching sounds the same in both layers and across all the songs. Only an everyday word (a boat, a hut, a cord) may be sung with a different English word when the approved one will not sing; say so in that couplet's `kept`. "Says" in the poet's self-naming stays "says". Never use a rejected term.
9. **`refrainCue`**: the first three to six words of your sung refrain, exactly as they stand in its first line, to be printed after each later couplet as the cue to sing it again (the source marks this with ধ্রু). If the unit has no refrain, "".
10. **Say what you did.** For every couplet, `kept` names in a sentence what the English keeps of the source's sound, image or feeling and how. `letGo` names what it gives up, if anything. Both 40 words or fewer, concrete, never self-praise. Put anything the reviewer must decide in `questions`.

Other published English versions of these songs exist and are under copyright. Do not reproduce them or reach for phrasing you remember from them. A sung version is where remembered phrasing creeps in most easily, so be strict: every line must come from the source and the accurate English in front of you.
