## 1. First, the rule that shapes everything: you write the prose

In March 2026, after a community RfC, English Wikipedia adopted a content guideline, [WP:NEWLLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Writing_articles_with_large_language_models):

> "The use of large language models (LLMs) to generate or rewrite article content is prohibited"

Its footnote names Claude among the tools covered. The rule applies to drafts too. Here is what that means for you:

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| Using AI to find sources and understand the topic, as in this project, and then **reading those sources yourself** ([WP:LLMRESEARCH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Responsibly_using_large_language_models#Research)) | Pasting, adapting or "rewording" AI-written text into the article, including anything in these files |
| Asking AI to point out **spelling, punctuation and grammar** mistakes in text *you* wrote. Save your own version first, then the corrections as a second edit, and say so in the edit summary ([WP:LLMCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Responsibly_using_large_language_models#Copyediting)) | AI-written talk-page posts, replies to reviewers, Teahouse questions or DYK hooks ([WP:AITALK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Talk_page_guidelines#LLM-generated)) |
| Asking AI to check your finished draft against the sources ("does p. 69 actually say this?") and to explain policies | Citing anything an AI wrote ([WP:RSML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Reliable_sources#WP:RSML)) |

Keep persistently breaking this rule and you get blocked ([WP:LLMDISRUPT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Disruptive_editing#Persistent_LLM_use)). AI-generated pages can also be speedily deleted ([WP:G15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Speedy_deletion#G15)). If anyone asks whether you used AI, answer honestly: you used it for research and source-finding, and you wrote the text yourself.

Honestly, this works in your favour. You said you wanted the experience, and the experience *is* the writing. Everything else has been done for you:
- the sources are found and checked;
- the facts are mapped to page numbers;
- the conflicts between sources are flagged;
- the policies are summarised.

## 2. Why he qualifies (notability)

Wikipedia's test is significant coverage in independent, reliable, secondary sources ([WP:GNG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability#General_notability_guideline), [WP:BASIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability_(people)#Basic_criteria), and [WP:ANYBIO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability_(people)#Any_biography) #2, "part of the enduring historical record"). You have more than enough:
- **A whole PhD dissertation about him:** Chien, University of Virginia, 2015. Chien then published two peer-reviewed journal articles from it (2016, 2020).
- **A 15th-century Tibetan history** that records him: the *Blue Annals*.
- **An academic review** that discusses his text: Garfield, *Journal of Buddhist Ethics*, 1996.
- **Book-length commentaries on his text** from established publishers (Shambhala, Snow Lion, Wisdom, LTWA) by Dilgo Khyentse, the Dalai Lama, the 17th Karmapa and others ([WP:AUTHOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability_(people)#Creative_professionals) #3).
- **A museum painting** of him (Rubin Museum) and a biography in The Treasury of Lives.

AfC reviewers usually look for **three strong, independent sources**. Build the article on Chien, the Blue Annals and Garfield. Add The Heart of Compassion for the traditional account, attributed as such.

## 3. The plan, phase by phase

### Phase 0: account and practice (days 1–5)

1. **Create an account.** Choose a personal username, not "37Practices" or anything that looks like an organisation ([WP:ORGNAME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Username_policy#Promotional_names)).
2. **Disclose your connection on your user page, in your own words.** Say that you run 37practices.space and a study-group initiative about the text, and that you're not paid to edit. Add `{{User COI|Draft:Gyalse Tokme Zangpo}}`.
   - Your conflict of interest (COI) is mild: it's about the *text*, not about a 14th-century monk. Disclosing costs nothing and builds trust with reviewers ([WP:DISCLOSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Conflict_of_interest#How_to_disclose_a_COI)).
3. **Make at least 10 small, genuine edits over at least 4 days.** That makes you "autoconfirmed" ([WP:AUTOCONFIRM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:User_groups#Autoconfirmed_users)), which you need for later steps such as creating redirects. Good first edits:
   - **Fix a real error.** The [Bodhicitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhicitta) article's "Source texts" list says *Thirty-Seven Practices* is "12th century CE". It is 14th century.
     - Correct it and cite Chien 2015 (p. 1 says "fourteenth-century").
     - Explain the change in the edit summary.
     - Because this touches your area of interest, keep it strictly factual.
   - **Typo and link fixes** in articles you read anyway (Lojong, Langri Tangpa, Chekawa Yeshe Dorje, Tai Situ Changchub Gyaltsen).
   - **Try the [Wikipedia Adventure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:The_Wikipedia_Adventure)**, a one-hour interactive tutorial.

### Phase 1: read (about 1–2 weeks)

Follow **[reading-guide.md](reading-guide.md)**. The minimum:
- Chien 2015, pp. 1–3, 12–18, 53–78, 80–81, 154–155 and 186–203 (free PDF). **This is the heart of it.**
- *The Heart of Compassion*: translators' introduction and the biography excerpt (pp. 9–26). Get it from a library, the Google Books preview, or your own shelf.
- *Blue Annals*: pp. 310, 584–585 and 1024 (free scan).
- Garfield 1996, p. 166 (free PDF).
- Pearcey's translation, with its colophon (Lotsawa House).

As you read, keep your own notes: fact, then source, then page number. **Your** notes, in **your** words, become the draft.

### Phase 2: write the draft (about 1–3 weeks)

1. **Start the draft.** Use the [Article Wizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Article_wizard) or go directly to `Draft:Gyalse Tokme Zangpo` (see §4 on the title).
2. **Write section by section** using the outline in §5. Aim for about 1,500–3,000 words of prose. Quality beats length.
3. **Cite as you go, with a page number on every book citation.** The quickest way is the editor's **Cite → Automatic** button: paste the DOI, ISBN or URL, then check the output against [sources.md](sources.md).
4. **Add the finishing pieces:** the infobox, the short description, `{{Authority control}}` and the Tibetan name template. Parameter names are in §7.
5. **Don't add categories yet.** Put them in as `[[:Category:1295 births]]` with a leading colon, or leave them out; the reviewer adds them on acceptance.
6. **Put `{{Connected contributor}}` on the draft's talk page.** Fill in your username, `U1-declared=yes`, and a short note about the website.

### Phase 3: submit (then wait 4+ weeks)

- **Run the pre-submission checklist** in §9.
- **Submit.** Click "Submit the draft for review!" in the yellow box, or add `{{subst:submit}}` at the top. Saving the page ("Publish changes") does **not** submit it.
- **Expect to wait.** The queue on 26 September 2026 was **2,223 drafts**, and the notice says "4 weeks or more". Reviews happen in no fixed order.
- **Answer reviewer comments yourself,** on the draft or its talk page. If a reviewer declines, read the reason, fix it and resubmit; that's normal. Questions go to the [AfC Help desk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:WikiProject_Articles_for_creation/Help_desk) or the [Teahouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Teahouse).
- **Don't move the draft into mainspace yourself.** With a declared COI, go through AfC.

### Phase 4: after acceptance (the same week)

- [ ] **Did You Know:** nominate for the Main Page's "Did you know…" **within 7 days** of acceptance.
  - You need more than 1,500 characters of prose, and the hook fact must be cited at the end of its sentence.
  - Your first five nominations don't require a review in return.
  - Write the hook yourself. The Tara/Bodong É story or the lice story would make good hooks.
- [ ] **Wikidata:** add the English Wikipedia sitelink to [Q16346532](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q16346532), so the German, French and Tibetan articles link to yours.
- [ ] **Redirects** (you need autoconfirmed status, or use [WP:AFC/R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Articles_for_creation/Redirects)). Create:
  - Tokme Zangpo, Thogme Zangpo, Tokmé Zangpo, Thogmé Zangpo, Thokmé Zangpo, Togmay Sangpo, Togme Zangpo
  - Gyelse Tokme Zangpo, Gyalsé Tokmé Zangpo, Gyalse Thogme Zangpo

### Later: an article on the text itself

The *Thirty-Seven Practices* deserves its own article too. The many independent commentaries meet [WP:NBOOK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability_(books)#Criteria) #1, and French Wikipedia has one (Wikidata [Q25668929](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q25668929)).

Be extra careful there: your conflict of interest is closest to the text.

## 4. Title and names

Wikipedia's [naming convention for Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Naming_conventions_(Tibetan)) says: use the spelling most familiar to English readers, "as supported by the weight of reliable sources". Include Wylie transliteration, but not in the title.

**Recommended title: `Gyalse Tokme Zangpo`, with no accents.**
- "Tokme Zangpo" is the core of the spelling used by the main scholarly source (Chien), The Treasury of Lives, the Rubin Museum and Lotsawa House's translation.
- "Gyalse" is how most English publications write the epithet, and it's how you know him.

A reviewer may prefer plain "Tokme Zangpo", since "Gyalse" is an honorific and the Manual of Style discourages honorifics ([MOS:HONORIFIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Biography#Honorifics)). If one moves the page, that's fine; the redirects cover every spelling.

**In the article:**
- **First sentence:** bold name, then `{{bo|t=རྒྱལ་སྲས་ཐོགས་མེད་བཟང་པོ|w=rgyal sras thogs med bzang po}}`, then (1295–1369).
- **Variant spellings:** put the long list in a footnote rather than cluttering the first sentence ([MOS:FOREIGNEQUIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Lead_section#Inclusion_of_foreign_equivalents)). reading-guide.md lists the variants that actually appear in your sources.
- **Short form:** after the first mention, "Tokme Zangpo" (or just "Tokme") is fine. Be consistent.
- **Diacritics:** they're optional. Pick with or without accents and stay consistent.

## 5. Suggested outline (headings and what goes under them)

This is a structure, not text. Page pointers for every item are in reading-guide.md, under the same headings.

1. **Lead** (2–3 short paragraphs, written *last*). Summarise the body:
   - who he was;
   - dates and place;
   - what he's known for (the 37 Practices);
   - the one or two most important facts about his life and works.
   - Leave out anything that isn't in the body.
2. **Names**: birth name, novice name, how he got "Tokme" and "Gyalse", why "Ngulchu", and the variant spellings (in a footnote).
3. **Life**
   - *Sources for his life*: the three early biographies, and a note that they are hagiographic. This short subsection makes the rest easy to write neutrally.
   - *Early life and education*
   - *Tara monastery and Bodong É*: follow the early biographies, and footnote the conflicting "abbot of Bodong É" claim.
   - *Retreat at Ngulchu and later life*
   - *Death*
4. **Reputation for compassion**, or fold this into Life. Attribute every story: "According to his earliest biography…".
5. **Works**
   - *Collected works*: the four editions and the 113/114 texts.
   - *Commentaries*: on the Bodhicaryāvatāra, the Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra and the Ratnagotravibhāga.
   - *Mind-training writings*: including his lineage text, with Chien's argument attributed to her.
   - *The Thirty-Seven Practices of Bodhisattvas*: composition, structure, reception and commentaries.
6. **Legacy**: how Sakya, Kadam and Gelug each claim him; his students; the site today; the painting.
7. Then **Notes**, **References**, **Further reading**, **External links**, `{{Authority control}}` and categories, in that order ([MOS:ORDER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Layout#Order_of_article_elements)).

## 6. Writing rules reviewers actually check

- **Attribute hagiography ([WP:NPOV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Neutral_point_of_view)).** Miracles, visions, emanations and the childhood stories go in as "According to [biographer]…" or "His biographer Palden Yeshe relates…", never as plain fact. Don't put "emanation of Avalokiteśvara" in the infobox.
- **No puffery ([MOS:PEACOCK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Words_to_watch#Puffery)).** Avoid "very important", "renowned", "great master", "revered", "legendary" and "profound". Show importance with facts instead: who wrote commentaries, where it's taught, what scholars say, attributed to them. Example: "Garfield notes that the text is unusual in explaining and justifying each practice."
- **No original research ([WP:NOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:No_original_research)).** Don't interpret the verses yourself. Describe the text only in plain terms (how many verses, what they cover) and leave analysis to published sources. Your own experience teaching the text can't go in.
- **Honorifics ([MOS:HONORIFIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Biography#Honorifics)).** Write "the 14th Dalai Lama", not "His Holiness", and "Garchen Rinpoche" only because that's his article title.
- **Copyright.** Don't paste verses from English translations. Pearcey's licence (CC BY-NC) is **not** compatible with Wikipedia's CC BY-SA. A one-line quoted, attributed excerpt is fine; whole verses are not.
- **Close paraphrase ([WP:CLOP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Close_paraphrasing)).** Read, close the source, then write. Don't rearrange a source's sentences.
- **Your website.** Don't cite or link 37practices.space anywhere, and don't mention the study groups. See [sources.md](sources.md), section 5.

## 9. Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Every sentence was written by me, from sources I read myself.
- [ ] Every paragraph ends with a citation, every book citation has a page number, and I have opened each page to confirm it says what my sentence says.
- [ ] Every ISBN or DOI resolves (check ISBNs on [WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/)).
- [ ] The dates are 1295–1369 everywhere. None of the web errors from reading-guide.md ("1297–1371", "Silver River", "Nyingma master") crept in.
- [ ] The Bodong É claim is handled neutrally: early biographies in the text, the other claim in a footnote with its citation. I read The Treasury of Lives and Rubin pages in my browser before citing them.
- [ ] Hagiographic material is attributed, and there are no miracles in Wikipedia's own voice.
- [ ] There are no peacock words, no "His Holiness", and no promotional tone.
- [ ] There is no link to or mention of 37practices.space or the study groups.
- [ ] There are no whole verses from copyrighted translations.
- [ ] The lead only summarises the body, and the first sentence follows [MOS:FIRSTBIO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Biography#Opening_paragraph).
- [ ] The infobox has no empty or placeholder fields.
- [ ] The preview shows no red errors.
- [ ] `{{Connected contributor}}` is on the draft's talk page, and my user page discloses the connection.
- [ ] There are no categories yet (or only colon-prefixed ones).
