## Life

### Biographical sources
- three early biographies, all by people who knew him (C p. 12):
  - *Guide to Your Devotion*: Zhönnu Gyaltsen Palzangpo
  - *Ray of Altruism*: Pelgyi Rinchen
  - *Drops of Ambrosia*: Palden Yeshe (disciple)
- ⚑ Chien: *Guide* probably the earliest; possibly 1374 (C pp. 12, 18)
- later lives: Künga Gyaltsen (1432–1506), Kadam history; Yeshe Gyaltsen (1713–1793), Lamrim lineage biographies (C p. 49)
- genre: namtar = biography plus hagiography (C p. 3)
- English excerpts of Palden Yeshe, translated by Matthieu Ricard (HoC translators' introduction)

### Early life and education
- born 1295, wood-sheep year (C p. 55)
- birthplace: Drakya near Sakya Monastery (C p. 54); Palden Yeshe gives Phuljung, "a few miles to the southwest" (HoC)
- parents Könchok Pal and Bumdrön; ⚑ ancestors local officials; grandfather's uncle an attendant of Chögyal Phagpa (C p. 54)
- mother died when he was 3, father when he was 5; grandmother until 9; then uncle Rinchen Tashi (C p. 58); the uncle taught him reading and writing (HoC)
- novice vows at 14, 1308; preceptor from Samling monastery (C p. 60)
- studied mainly at Samling, Bodong É and Sakya; summer debate tours incl. Shalu (C pp. 60, 65)
- Abhidharmasamuccaya: five teachers, five years; Prajñāpāramitā; Kadam teachings (C p. 65)
- ⚑ the "Tokme" name: about age 20, Bodong É, answered an Abhidharmakośa question, won the debate; "not a new Tokmé" (C pp. 65–66); Palden Yeshe: "second Asanga" at 19 (HoC)
- about 30 teachers; subjects: Abhidharma, epistemology, Asaṅga and Vasubandhu, bodhicitta rituals, tantric empowerments (C p. 67)
- Mahāmudrā and the Six Yogas at Ngulchu, despite little Kagyu affiliation (C p. 67)
- guru yoga from Jamyang Dönyö Gyaltsen; Seven-Point Mind Training from Sönam Drakpa (C p. 67)
- Eight Verses from Lozangpa; Bodhicaryāvatāra twice (C pp. 67–68)
- spiritual guide at Sakya at 27; studies complete by 32 (C p. 68)
- full ordination at Bodong É, about 1325 (C p. 86)
- footnote: age at full ordination 30 (C p. 68) vs 29 (HoC); Tibetan age counting

### Tara monastery and Bodong É
- age 32 (about 1326): instructor at Bodong É; the same year head of Tara (*rta ra*) at the monks' request (C pp. 68–69)
- reluctant; agreed on a promise of little administration (C p. 69)
- length of tenure: seven years, 1326–1333 (BDRC); until age 41 (HoC)
- location of Tara unknown (C p. 69 n. 132)
- autumn 1333: offered the Bodong É abbacy by Jamyang Dönyö Gyaltsen; declined; proposed his teacher Pang Lotsāwa Lodrö Tenpa (1276–1342), who accepted (C p. 69)
- Bodong É monks still told the story in 2013 (C pp. 1, 69–70)
- footnote: some reference works say "abbot of Bodong É 1326–1335". Check the Treasury of Lives in your browser before citing.

### Retreat at Ngulchu and later years
- Ngulchu Chödzong about 40 miles NE of Bodong É (C p. 67 n. 124)
- until 1337 mainly at Bodong É and Ngulchu; from 1337, retreat of about 20 years; rarely met people (C p. 70)
- left rarely, e.g. to visit Pang Lotsāwa before Pang's death in 1342 (C p. 70)
- 1361, age 67: travels to Samye, the Jokhang and other sites; back in 1363 (C p. 70)
- figures who invited him to teach: Butön, Tai Situ Changchub Gyaltsen, Lama Dampa Sönam Gyaltsen, Tai Situ Gelek Zangpo (C p. 71). Not tied to specific years.
- Phagmodru hierarchs heard teachings from him (BA pp. 584–585)

### Death
- died 1369 at Ngulchu Chödzong (C p. 202)
- BA: earth-bird year 1369 (BA p. 310)
- ⚑ Palden Yeshe: seated in the lotus posture from one morning to the next evening; cremation; relics in reliquaries and statues (HoC)
- no reincarnation line (C p. 203)

## Portrayal in the biographies
- ⚑ *Guide* portrays him as an ethical model for lojong practitioners (C pp. 70, 73)
- ⚑ generosity stories: beggars, a crippled woman, dogs, bandits (C p. 58)
- ⚑ the lice story: beggar taken in; given food and a new fur cloak; he took the lice-ridden clothes; fell ill; refused to give up the lice; his words (quote C p. 72); about 17 days; afterwards known as "Great Bodhisattva" (C pp. 71–73)
- ⚑ Chien: parallel with Butön's account of Asaṅga and the worm-infested dog; possible model for the story (C pp. 72–73)
- ⚑ Chien: birth miracles are stock motifs (C p. 55); asked about the childhood stories, he said he couldn't remember them (C p. 57)
- ⚑ Chien: prominent patrons gained prestige from the association; he built a network (C p. 71)
- ⚑ Chien: subject, biographer and community together shaped his identity (C p. 202)
- ⚑ Palden Yeshe's epithet "second Avalokiteśvara" (C p. 195)
- ⚑ optional: the robbers story (HoC)
- to strengthen this section: Chien 2020 (*IJBTC*), which is about exactly this

## Works

### *The Thirty-Seven Practices of Bodhisattvas*
- Tibetan title *rgyal sras lag len so bdun ma*; text 29 in the Degé edition; "Thirty-Seven" not in the original title (C p. 154 n. 301)
- ⚑ composition story: urged to give paid tantric empowerments; declined; wrote the text; the wind scattered the pages in a ravine; rewrote it (C p. 154)
- Palden Yeshe also links the composition to a time of hardship (HoC)
- colophon: Jewel Cave (Rinchen Puk), Ngulchu (P)
- structure: homage and statement of purpose; 37 four-line verses, each with the refrain "the practice of all the bodhisattvas"; four closing verses, the last a dedication; colophon (P; count it yourself)
- ⚑ Garfield: emphasis on morality and altruism; practices suited to lay people; explains and justifies each practice (G p. 166)
- ⚑ Padmakara translators: considered a distillation of the Bodhicaryāvatāra (HoC translators' introduction)
- the most discussed of his works (C p. 154)
- no recorded commentary 15th–18th century; eight Tibetan commentaries from the 19th–21st centuries (C p. 155 n. 305)
- modern teachers: Dilgo Khyentse, Geshe Jampa Tegchok, the 14th Dalai Lama, the 17th Karmapa (C p. 2)
- the Dalai Lama taught it for three days at Bodh Gaya in 1974 (G p. 166)
- Minyak Kunzang Sönam (Chökyi Drakpa), disciple of Patrul: commentary in English as *Uniting Wisdom and Compassion* (HoC; Chökyi Dragpa 2004)

### Mind-training writings
- two commentaries on the Seven-Point Mind Training (C p. 191)
- text 9, "Lineage of Mahāyāna Lojong Masters": Buddha → Maitreya → Asaṅga → Vasubandhu → … → Atiśa → the Kadampas → Sönam Drakpa (C pp. 186–187). Don't give a count of masters.
- ⚑ Chien: shaped by Kadam masters and texts; its distinctive feature is the inclusion of masters associated with Asaṅga (C p. ii, pp. 191–192)
- no Sakya master in the list despite his Sakya affiliation (C p. 186)

### Scholastic commentaries
- Bodhicaryāvatāra: *legs par bshad pa'i rgya mtsho* (C p. 189 n. 372); English translation in *The Bodhisattva Ideal* (Wisdom, 2026). Check the book first.
- Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra: *rin po che'i phreng ba* (C p. 189 n. 371)
- Ratnagotravibhāga/Uttaratantra: *nges don gsal bar byed pa'i 'od zer* (C p. 192 n. 379)
- an Abhidharmasamuccaya commentary mentioned in all three biographies, not found (C p. 192 n. 377)

### Collected works
- four editions: Thimphu 1975; a Drepung manuscript; the 18th-century Degé xylograph; Labrang (Lanzhou 2011) (C pp. 80–81)
- a common core of 113 texts; Degé and Labrang add a 114th, *Great Hearing Transmission of Mind Training* (C p. 81)
- text 114: part 1 by Palden Yeshe, part 2 by Tokme Zangpo (C p. 194)
- ⚑ Chien: Palden Yeshe and the Fifth Dalai Lama shaped his image as a lojong master through editing (C p. 203)

## Legacy
- Sakya affiliation per his biographies (C p. 186)
- included in a Kadam history (Künga Gyaltsen) and in the Gelug Lamrim lineage biographies (Yeshe Gyaltsen) (C p. 49)
- ⚑ Chien: remembered because later scholars such as the Fifth Dalai Lama placed him in the lojong lineage (C p. 203)
- students: Rendawa Zhönnu Lodrö (1349–1412), Palden Yeshe, others (BDRC)
- an Amoghapāśa (Avalokiteśvara) transmission lineage (BA p. 1024)
- Ngulchu Chödzong today: Xaitongmoin (Xietongmen) County, Shigatse; a shrine with his statue opposite meditation caves; local veneration seen in 2013 (C p. 1)
- painting: Rubin Museum, 18th century, *Gyelse Tokme Zangpo (1295–1369) and scenes from his life*, C2002.4.1 (Rubin page, for the painting only)
